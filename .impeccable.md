## Design Context

### Users

tinys is for everyday people using fun, focused mini apps and documents on their desktop. Each tiny should make one job immediately understandable and fast: count something, prepare an invoice, follow a recipe, run a timer, capture a note, or operate another small personal tool.

### Brand Personality

Clear, tactile, expressive. Interfaces should feel approachable and delightful without making users decode novelty. Ease of use comes first; personality supports the purpose.

### Aesthetic Direction

Treat every tiny as a complete digital object rather than a miniature website. Use compact composition, shallow layered depth, direct manipulation, and one memorable purpose-specific detail. Paper-like documents should be restrained and precise; instruments can use inset displays and tactile controls; fully skinned tinys can adopt sculpted silhouettes when the form reinforces the function. Avoid generic dashboard grids, decorative gradients, excessive pills, glass effects, and interchangeable rounded cards.

### Design Principles

- Make the single purpose and primary action obvious at a glance.
- Build depth from at most three meaningful layers: chassis or backing, primary surface, and inset or raised controls.
- Let each tiny's purpose determine its typography, palette, and degree of expression; consistency comes from clarity and interaction quality.
- Prefer compact direct editing, accessible primitives, visible focus, reduced motion, and layouts that adapt rather than merely shrink.
- Keep exportable content in document flow and explicitly remove editing-only controls from static captures.
